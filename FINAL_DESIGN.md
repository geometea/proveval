# Final Design

## Research question

How does claimed provenance affect an LLM judge's evaluation of fixed prose?

In particular, distinguish:

1. user-specific authorship effects
2. general human-authorship effects
3. AI-authorship effects

## Status

All existing results are exploratory pilot data.

The final study design is frozen before collecting any new final-study
results. Pilot observations will not be pooled into the final-study
analysis.

## Corpus

Target 12 distinct prose texts.

The four current stories may be included, but the final corpus must contain
substantially more underlying texts than the pilot.

All provenance statements are experimentally manipulated metadata and need
not reflect true authorship.

Keep texts approximately comparable in length where practical.

## Experimental unit

Use target-anchor comparisons rather than all possible story pairs.

Each of the 12 texts serves as a target in two predetermined target-anchor
pairings, giving 24 target-anchor units.

For each target-anchor unit:

- keep the underlying target text fixed
- keep the anchor text fixed
- keep target/anchor A-B position fixed across provenance conditions
- balance target position globally so roughly half of units have target=A
  and half target=B

## Provenance conditions

Apply provenance metadata only to the target story.

Use four target conditions:

**neutral:**
"The following short story is written in English."

**self:**
"I wrote this short story."

**other_human:**
"This short story was written by another person."

**ai:**
"This short story was generated by an AI."

The anchor receives the same neutral metadata in every condition.

## Evaluation prompt

For the first final-study run, preserve the current evaluator instruction,
including the instruction to base ratings on the text itself.

Do not introduce a standard-vs-debiased prompt manipulation yet.

## Replicates

Run exactly 3 replicates per target-anchor-condition combination.

24 target-anchor units × 4 provenance conditions × 3 replicates = 288
primary comparison observations.

Do not run more replicates after inspecting results without documenting
that as a separate follow-up experiment.

## Primary outcome

Convert pairwise preference to a target-relative score so that positive
always means preference for the target, regardless of whether target is
Story A or B.

For each target-anchor unit and replicate, compare the same underlying
target under:

- AI vs self
- other_human vs self
- AI vs other_human

Primary contrasts:

1. AI − self
2. other_human − self
3. AI − other_human

Interpretation:

- **AI > self but other_human ≈ AI:** possible user-specific disadvantage.
- **AI > self and AI > other_human:** general AI-provenance advantage.
- **other_human > self while AI ≈ other_human:** user-specific
  social-context effect.

## Secondary outcomes

For the target story's five dimension ratings, compute the analogous
contrasts:

- plot_structure
- prose_style
- characterization
- originality
- overall_quality

## Controls

Include an identical-text negative-control set in the final corpus.

For each of the 12 texts, compare the text against an identical copy once
under:

- self_vs_ai
- ai_vs_self

This gives 24 final-study control observations.

These controls are run once, not three times.

**Expected final-study total:** 288 primary observations + 24 identical-text
controls = 312 API observations.

## Analysis

Primary analysis must operate on fixed target-anchor units, not treat every
API call as an independent prose sample.

Report:

- mean provenance contrasts
- distribution of positive / zero / negative within-unit shifts
- results by target text
- results by replicate
- component-rating contrasts
- identical-text control tie rate

Use uncertainty estimates clustered/bootstrap-resampled by target text or
target-anchor unit rather than treating repeated model calls as independent
texts.

Do not calculate final statistical tests until the analysis implementation
is specified before data collection.

## Pilot result motivating the design

The pilot found:

- 20/20 identical-text controls tied
- journal-vs-AI had approximately zero aggregate effect
- self-vs-AI showed an exploratory AI-label advantage
- the pilot could not distinguish AI preference from user-authorship effects

These findings motivated, but are not part of, the final study.
